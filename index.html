What could go wrong

Product

People don't use it, like Play Something

The banner shows up at the moment of choice. Kill criterion: if under 10% use it again after the test, we pause.

Tech

End times are wrong

End-credits markers, rounding up to 5 minutes, and no titles without reliable runtime data.

UX

Weak options in short windows

Only titles that pass a relevance bar, and a clear "nothing fits" screen instead of filler.

Business

People watch less overall

Guardrails on evening viewing and retention. "Keep watching" is always one click away.

Rollout

Launch problems across teams

Employees first, then 1%, 10%, and the 50/50 test. A feature flag with instant rollback.

What we learned

The hardest trade-off

Leaving out the ads plan, even though it brings most new sign-ups. Without predictable ad breaks, we can't promise an end time.

The decision we'd defend first

Measuring the whole test group, not only the people who use the feature. Otherwise we'd measure who chooses it, not what it does.

What sketching changed

Two questions the PRD didn't answer: watching an episode again, and an episode that almost fits. We added two requirements and one clear rule.

What we'd validate first

How often people really stop mid-title, the 60% completion baseline, and whether 15% try it within a quarter. Each is an assumption the plan rests on.

Why the first release is this size

Smaller would break the promise. Bigger would delay the test that tells us if the promise works.

It can't be smaller

Without episode runs, the promise breaks for most viewing: series are about 3 of every 4 hours. A films-only version would copy the short-movie row that already exists.

Without personalization, it's just a runtime filter. Play Something showed people want relevant options, not any title.

Without an end time on every option, the member still does the math, and the feature becomes one more row.

Without the "nothing fits" screen, short late-evening windows show empty or weak results, exactly for the people who need it most.

It shouldn't be bigger

The ads plan needs a prediction of ad breaks for each member. A wrong end time breaks the core promise, so it waits for that prediction.

Film-and-episode mixes and watching together add real logic and screen complexity before we know that single options solve the problem.

Every extra platform or plan delays the A/B test. With a fixed team and a one-quarter target, scope is the thing we control.

How we estimated reach

Reach is the number of eligible members we expect to use the feature in a quarter. Every step after the first is our assumption, kept on the low side.

How we measure

Three metrics, one test, two guardrails. Baselines marked as assumptions are replaced by Netflix's own numbers before launch.

The test. Ad-free members on the TV app in the US and Canada, split 50/50, for four weeks.

Success. The primary goal is met with statistical significance, plus at least one secondary goal.

Guardrails. Total evening viewing hours and 28-day retention in the test group must not fall below control.

Why 2% and not 25%. Inside sessions that use the feature we expect starts about 25% faster. Most sessions won't use it, so across the whole group the effect is about 2%.

Events we track. Feature opened, time selected, options shown, option selected, playback started, planned run completed, left without playing.

Quality requirements and pricing

How the feature has to behave, beyond what it does, and how it is packaged.

Quality

Speed. Options on screen within 2 seconds for 95% of requests. The banner adds no more than 100 ms to Home.

Reliability. If personalization doesn't answer within 3 seconds, a list by length and local popularity appears. Never a blank screen, and a failure here never affects browsing or playback.

Accuracy. The end time is right within 5 minutes for at least 95% of options.

Ease. At most 3 remote clicks to playing. Existing TV patterns, readable from the couch, full screen-reader support.

Privacy. Only data Netflix already uses. No new personal data. The last time picked is saved per profile.

Local rules and compatibility. Only licensed titles allowed by maturity settings. A 12- or 24-hour clock by device. Older TV app versions simply don't show the entry.

Pricing

Price. No change and no separate charge.

Packaging. Included in every ad-free plan in the launch markets. Not on the ads plan at launch.

Paid add-on. Rejected. It shrinks reach and adds friction to a feature built to remove it.

The ads plan. Not marketed as a reason to leave it: it drove more than 60% of sign-ups in its markets in Q1 2026. The exclusion is technical and temporary.

Business value. Indirect. Faster starts and finished evenings support engagement and retention.

How members get it. No purchase flow. It appears for eligible profiles through the evening banner.
