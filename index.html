Risks

What could go wrong, and what we do about it.

Product

People don't use it, like Play Something

The banner appears at the moment of choice. If under 10% use it again after the test, we pause.

Tech

End times are wrong

Count to the end credits, round up, and skip titles without reliable runtime data.

UX

Weak options in short windows

Relevant titles only, and a clear "nothing fits" screen instead of filler.

Business

People watch less overall

Guardrails on evening viewing and retention. "Keep watching" is one click away.

Rollout

Launch problems across teams

Employees first, then 1%, 10% and the 50/50 test, with instant rollback.

What we learned

The hardest trade-off

Leaving out the ads plan, even though it brings most new sign-ups. Without predictable ad breaks, we can't promise an end time.

The decision we'd defend first

Measuring the whole test group, not only people who use the feature. Otherwise we'd measure who chooses it, not what it does.

What we'd check first

How often people stop mid-title, the 60% completion baseline, and whether 15% try it. The plan rests on these three assumptions.

How we estimated reach

Appendix

Reach is the number of eligible members we expect to try the feature in a quarter. Every step after the first is our assumption, kept on the low side.

Paid memberships in the US and Canada, end of 202489.6M
× on ad-free plans (50%)44.8M
× watching on TV in the evening (70%) = eligible~31M
× try it within a quarter (15%) = reach≈ 4.7M

Bars are to scale. Membership has grown since 2024, so the real number is likely higher.

Impact scale. 3 massive · 2 high · 1 medium · 0.5 low · 0.25 minimal.

The whole launch scores about 0.59: 4.7M × 2 × 50% ÷ 8 months. Confidence is only 50% because Play Something failed on adoption.

Effort. 6.5 months of work for the four launch items, 8 once tracking, the A/B setup and testing across TV devices are added.

The test. Ad-free members on the TV app in the US and Canada, split 50/50, for four weeks.

Success. The primary goal is met with statistical significance, plus at least one secondary goal.

Guardrails. Total evening viewing hours and 28-day retention in the test group must not fall below control.

Why 2% and not 25%. Inside sessions that use the feature we expect starts about 25% faster. Most sessions won't use it, so across the whole group the effect is about 2%.

Events we track. Feature opened, time selected, options shown, option selected, playback started, planned run completed, left without playing.
